PBUA means Post-Binary Unified Architecture. It is a proposed way to move pictures, sound, and large computer data without
forcing every heavy job through a single-file stream of ordinary 0s and 1s. The computers themselves can stay ordinary. 
The camera can stay ordinary. The speaker and screen can stay ordinary. What changes is the transport layer: information
is organized as an A–Z photonic alphabet, sent through many wavelengths of light when light is available, and kept alive 
by radio and electrical links when light is weak. Version 2 made that dual-domain rule honest. Version 2.1 keeps that 
honesty and adds a third engine so the alphabet does not vanish the moment the beam sags.Call the rebuilt system 
PBUA 2.1, the Tri-Domain Fabric. Its public name is Optical Standard, Wireless Bonded, Electrical Efficient. 
Optical light is the preferred bulk road. Radio is no longer only the emergency exit. A thin electrical path 
remains for old cables and for low-energy control. A new engine named SE-40 keeps one A–Z session across all 
three roads. EL-40 still watches heat, power, errors, and deadlines. The project window is 2026–2030.
The status is build-me phase. PBUA is not an industry standard.A newcomer should first drop a common myth.
This system does not claim that photons outrun light, or that a phone becomes a rainbow computer, or that
every transistor starts thinking in twenty-six colors. Binary logic can remain inside chips. “Post-binary”
here means the highway is allowed to carry richer symbols than one bit at a time. An A–Z letter is a logical identity. 
In hardware it is usually a locked infrared wavelength, not a crayon from a child’s rainbow. Diagrams may paint those
lanes as colors so people can see them. The physical lanes may live near 1310 to 1550 nanometers, where silicon and
silicon-nitride photonics already work.Version 2 taught a hard physics lesson and it still stands. Do not force
twenty-six optical wavelengths into ordinary radio spectrum. Light and radio are both electromagnetic radiation, 
so people may call both of them photons, but the hardware is different. Light in a waveguide uses WDM and PAM. 
Radio uses QAM, MCS, MIMO, OFDMA, carrier aggregation, and multi-link Wi-Fi or cellular. Version 2.1 does not
undo that split. It only refuses Version 2’s extra political split: “if the optical signal is weak, PBUA is
over and the user now lives in 4G or 5G.” That collapse was the dispute. Version 2.1 answers it.The dispute was simple.
When the photonic signal was not strong enough, Version 2 lessened the post-binary framework down to normal fourth- 
and fifth-generation availability. Compatibility survived. The A–Z session did not. Version 2.1 keeps the session. 
The letters can change vehicles. They do not lose their names.Imagine a book being copied across town. Version 2 said: 
send the whole book by fiber if the fiber is perfect; otherwise throw the book on a cell network and hope. 
Version 2.1 says: keep the same book title and page numbers. Send the heavy pages by light. Send the table of 
contents, the audio track, the spare copies of fragile pages, and any overflow pages by radio at the same time.
If the fiber dims, more pages ride radio. The reader at the far end still binds one book. 
That binder is SE-40.SE-40 is the third engine. EL-40 remains the governor of energy and deadlines. 
SE-40 is the clerk of identity. It stamps every block with a session ID, a frame ID, a symbol-group ID, 
a timestamp, and an energy class. It maps those groups onto whichever physical engine is healthy. 
It holds a short electron packet at the edge, then writes that packet onto a brief colored or
infrared photon burst so two engines can be lined up in time. That hold lasts nanoseconds to microseconds. 
It is not a photon hard drive. Electrons store. Photons carry. Then the hold is cleared.
That short electron-on-photon encode is one of the upgrades recovered from the small-component conversation.
A custom edge head can look as humble as a CD pickup and still serve PBUA. A CD player already uses a small
infrared diode and a photodiode that asks only bright or dark. Version 2.1 keeps one such dumb infrared lane 
as a compatibility heartbeat. Around it, a patterned quantum-dot film or a filtered micro-comb can emit a
few strong, separated peaks. Each peak is a pad with its own driver. Colors are not born in a mixed glow and 
hoped apart later. If two peaks smear, they are not two letters. They are one muddy error.How many colors?
Not a souvenir rainbow, and not two hundred and fifty-six ASCII crayons. The logical alphabet can stay A–Z,
twenty-six identities, about 4.70 bits per ideal letter before overhead. The physical grid should start at 
eight locked infrared data lanes plus one legacy on/off infrared lane. If those eight stay clean, grow to 
sixteen. Only then attempt twenty-six physical lines. Visible quantum-dot colors, four to eight of them, 
belong on the short dock or free-space head, never as the long-haul fabric. Guard bands matter more than 
poetry. A strong signal with fewer lanes beats a smeared twenty-six.The packing method stays the
Version 2 split because that split was the scientific rescue of the whole project. Radix is
how information is organized. WDM is how many wavelength lanes run at once. PAM is how many 
brightness steps ride on one lane. The system does not ask one laser to show twenty-six
fragile brightness levels. It asks many stable lanes to carry PAM-4 or, when the lane is
quiet, PAM-8. PAM-4 is the default adult mode. PAM-8 is a bonus. QAM stays on the radio side.
The first custom head does not try to read optical phase like a coherent fiber box.Bytes come 
from that packing, not from naming a color after a letter of English. Across eight PAM-4 lanes, 
one shared symbol time carries sixteen bits, which is two bytes. If those lanes someday run at
a high laboratory baud rate, raw optical capacity can climb into the hundreds of gigabits per 
second and, in later envelopes, toward a terabit-class design objective. Twenty-six clean PAM-4 lanes
at a high baud rate would be still larger. Those numbers are envelopes. Forward error correction, 
headers, heat, and packaging will eat a share. They are not a speed-test screenshot.Is the concept
faster than fifth-generation mobile networks? Conceptually yes on a short, clean optical 
path carrying a fat workload. Conceptually no as a phone in a pocket. Those two sentences must travel together.
Fifth-generation networks are real deployed radio systems. Everyday 5G latency is often tens of milliseconds.
Best-case 5G slogans talk about one millisecond on special air interfaces. Real-world 5G downloads are often
hundreds of megabits per second, with small urban spots able to reach much more. PBUA 2.1’s optical flight
across a room or a dock is only nanoseconds of travel. That flight is not the whole delay. Cameras, encoders,
error correction, and screens still live in milliseconds. The conceptual win is that a large frame need not
wait in one binary line, and that a flickering beam need not tear down the session and start a new 5G life.
Is it sixth generation or seventh? Not as an official badge. Sixth generation is the next mobile 
family being defined toward about 2030. Research targets talk about higher peak rates than 5G 
and user-plane latency often in the low milliseconds, sometimes tighter in special cases.
Seventh generation is not an ITU generation. PBUA 2.1 may sit beside 6G as a helper fabric.
It is not 6G, and it is not 7G.Why did earlier drafts say eighth generation? Because the 
authors wanted a name for an architecture that is not “another jump in radio frequency.” 
The 8G-class claim in this project means: high-radix symbols, many wavelengths at once, 
adaptive photonic transport, bonded radio, electrical compatibility, and a controller 
that schedules all of that as one media machine. It is a vision label. It is not a licensed 
telecom generation. Version 2.1 keeps the label only with that warning printed on the tin.
Is it less energy than fifth-generation internet to computers? Conceptually it can be, when 
a short optical or electrical path replaces a radio climb through the air and the core 
network for a large file or frame. Radio, especially cellular, is usually expensive in 
joules per bit. A docked optical lane can be cheaper per delivered bit if unused lasers sleep.
Version 2.1 makes that sleep rule stricter than Version 2. If the host only speaks HDMI or 
DisplayPort, the photonic palace turns off. Compatibility must not cost a hidden optical tax. 
If the host can take a hybrid stripe, light only the few strong lanes that carry the fat payload. 
The metric is joules per successfully presented frame, not the wattage of a proud transmitter.
The whole capability list is the union of original PBUA, Version 2, and the 2.1 bond. 
The system can accept a binary computer, a camera, a microphone, or a display. 
It can map their data into A–Z groups. It can send those groups through guided infrared waveguides,
through a short free-space optical beam or Li-Fi-style dock, through Wi-Fi or cellular radio, or through HDMI,
DisplayPort, eDP, PCIe-class, UCIe-class, Ethernet, or USB-class electrical pipes. It can reconstruct a normal
framebuffer and a normal audio buffer so the panel and the speaker never need to learn the alphabet.
It can timestamp capture and presentation separately. It can protect synchronization first, 
audio second, essential video third, and pretty enhancement last. It can degrade a session
in layers instead of dying. It can measure tail latency, not just average latency. 
It can turn wavelengths off. It can refuse PAM-8 when PAM-4 wastes less energy on retries.
Version 2 already contained the dual-domain ports: optical standard, free-space optical, 
wireless optional, legacy electrical, and a control sideband. Version 
2.1 keeps every port and changes their friendship. Wireless becomes a 
bonded helper while optical still runs. Electrical becomes a first-class low-energy citizen, not a consolation prize. 
The connector idea named PUGC remains: one family of plugs and modules that can hold optical, radio,
legacy copper, power, and sideband negotiation. The adapter remains the membrane between generations.
A 2026 design context still includes HDMI 2.2, DisplayPort and eDP families, PCIe 7.0, UCIe 3.0, 
Wi-Fi multi-link, and 5G with a future 6G hook. Those are neighbors, not proof that PBUA is itself
a standard.The controller pair is the new heart. EL-40 asks: which mix meets the deadline with the 
least energy and heat? SE-40 asks: how do I keep this one A–Z document intact while the mix changes? 
Together they replace Version 2’s serial ladder with a striped one. Optical bulk groups, radio
overflow and parity groups, and electrical control groups can exist in the same breath. 
The receiver merges by ticket, not by religion of PHY.Materials stay on the Version 2
path because that path was the manufacturing rescue. The bulk chip is silicon
photonics or silicon-nitride photonics. A micro-comb can grow many lines from one pump. 
A little III–V or other gain material may still be needed for the pump. The project 
does not return to exotic stacked crystals, polariton computers, or entanglement as a 
required feature. It does not call itself non-toxic. It only claims a lower-hazard 
direction than the earliest fantasy materials. Quantum dots appear at the small edge
head, where size-tuned emission can make a few strong colors for a dock, not as a 
claim that a visible rainbow is the long-distance network.Error correction stays 
layered: an outer Reed-Solomon-style shield and an inner soft-decision LDPC or 
polar-style shield are the working plan. A post-FEC bit-error target such as 10−1510^{-15}10^{-15}
 is a target. Wavelength lock, thermal feedback, guard bands, and the recover rule remain: remove a sick lane, 
 reassign its letters, recover the session. One dirty color must not poison the alphabet.A newcomer can now see
 what the system is capable of in daily language. It can carry a camera frame across a desk by light while the 
 same frame’s heartbeat rides radio. It can let a person stand up and walk, and the picture does not have to be
 reborn as a stranger on 5G. It can plug an old cable into an old screen and sleep the lasers. It can plug a
 future dock into a future panel and wake eight, sixteen, or someday twenty-six clean lanes. It can treat
 audio as sacred time. It can refuse to boast that twenty-six times the lanes means twenty-six times lower delay. 
 It can still claim, as a concept, less waiting in line for large payloads, less wasted energy when hardware sleeps,
 and a gentler failure than Version 2’s drop from photonic language to plain cellular.
 The build order is the honesty clause. First, software tickets and a fake A–Z mapper.
 Second, one optical lane with PAM and correction. Third, eight clean lanes that do not smear. 
 Fourth, SE-40 merge of optical groups with radio headers. Fifth, the custom diode and quantum-dot edge
 head with a legacy infrared blink. Sixth, the universal adapter and PUGC. Seventh, free-space docking.
 Eighth, a camera-to-speaker demonstration. Ninth, a bench comparison against ordinary 5G, Wi-Fi, and
 cable using the same movie and the same file. Until that ninth step exists, speed and energy remain 
 reasoned hopes.The one-line definition is now this. PBUA 2.1 is a proposed post-binary transport
 architecture in which an A–Z logical alphabet rides a tri-domain fabric: locked optical wavelengths
 with adaptive PAM for bulk data, bonded radio using ordinary QAM and cellular or Wi-Fi methods for
 helper and overflow symbols, and an electrical path that stays cheap when light is unused; EL-40 
 spends power only against a deadline; SE-40 keeps one ticketed session so a weak photonic signal
 no longer demotes the idea to fourth- or fifth-generation leftovers; and a short electron-to-photon 
 hold exists only long enough to line the engines up.That is the entire rebuilt concept. 
 It is still an idea. In concept it can be faster and leaner than fifth-generation internet when the job is a short,
 fat, mostly optical hop into a computer or a screen. In concept it is not a sixth, seventh, or
 official eighth generation of mobile radio. It is a photonic-first, radio-bonded, electrically
 polite fabric that uses the word 8G-class only to name its ambition: many letters of light, working with radio instead of surrendering to it.

Rules:Optical carries the heavy groups while its SNR stays above a hard floor.
Wireless does not wait for optical death. It already carries sync, audio, parity, and overflow letters.
When optical sag begins, SE-40 moves more groups to radio without closing the A–Z session.
Electrical path is not only “HDMI after failure.” It is a low-energy binary pipe for control and for hosts that never speak photonics.

That is faster than Version 2 fallback because you do not stall while switching worlds. The session never ends. Only the mix of engines changes.
