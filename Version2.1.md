forcing every heavy job through a single-file stream of ordinary 0s and 1s. The computers themselves can stay ordinary.

PBUA 2.1, the Tri-Domain Fabric. Its public name is Optical Standard, Wireless Bonded, Electrical Efficient. 
Optical light is the preferred bulk road. Radio is no longer only the emergency exit. A thin electrical path

That overlap is inter-channel crosstalk. The architecture therefore prefers fewer, well-separated carriers over a denser but unstable grid.Those carriers are generated in two places. The high-capacity fabric uses a silicon or silicon-nitride micro-comb. A pump source injects a resonator; four-wave mixing and related nonlinear processes split that pump into a comb of regularly spaced optical lines. Narrow filters and closed-loop thermal control select a usable subset and hold each line on its assigned frequency. The edge module uses semiconductor diodes and quantum dots. A diode is a p–n junction in which injected carriers recombine across a band gap and emit photons. Quantum dots add a size-quantized gap: confinement energy rises as the nanocrystal shrinks, so emission moves to shorter wavelength. In this design the dots are not a mixed film. They are patterned into separately driven pads, one size and one spectral peak per pad, so a channel is selected electrically rather than recovered from a blended spectrum.These components are not new particles invented for Version 2.1. Laser diodes, photodiodes, micro-combs, and quantum dots already exist. What is specific to 2.1 is the integration rule: one peak per emitter path, matched optical filters on the receiver row, a single legacy infrared on/off channel that an intensity detector can read without wavelength intelligence, and a short charge-to-photon encoder at the boundary. An electronic packet sets a modulator or dot state; that state imprints amplitude and wavelength onto a brief optical burst; the state is then cleared. Electrons remain the stored information. Photons are the transient carrier. No long-lived memory is claimed for the propagating field.Modulation stays intensity-based on the optical side. PAM-4 assigns four amplitude levels to each symbol and therefore two bits per symbol on a given wavelength. PAM-8 assigns eight levels and three bits, but only when signal-to-noise ratio and temperature keep the constellation separable. The first physical grid is eight locked infrared data channels plus one compatibility channel. Four to eight visible quantum-dot peaks may be added only on the short free-space or docking head. The logical alphabet can still contain twenty-six identities. Those identities time-share or group-share the smaller physical grid until sixteen, and later twenty-six, locked lines are shown to remain orthogonal.This spectral plan is what allows the dual path to operate as one session. SE-40 does not require an all-optical copy or an all-radio copy of a frame. It attaches a durable ticket to each symbol group—session, frame, group index, timestamp, energy class—and maps groups onto whichever physical layer currently meets the error and deadline budget. Guided infrared channels carry the high-volume groups while their signal remains above a defined floor. The radio path simultaneously carries synchronization, audio, parity, and overflow groups after translation into a native radio format such as QAM with MCS adaptation. That translation does not place the optical wavelength grid into radio spectrum. It preserves the tickets. When an optical channel fades, additional groups are steered onto radio without closing the A–Z session. The adapter reassembles one frame from mixed arrivals once enough ticketed groups exist.The older Version 2 sequence treated the radio path as a replacement after optical collapse. Version 2.1 treats it as a concurrent helper. The receiver’s completion condition is therefore not “every optical carrier arrived intact.” It is “the ticketed set is sufficient to reconstruct the frame.” A weakened photonic signal then means that some logical symbols have changed physical layer, not that the post-binary session has been discarded and replaced by an ordinary fourth- or fifth-generation link.

LEGAL OUTRO — PBUA 2.1 / TRI-DOMAIN FABRIC
Optical Standard · Wireless Bonded · Electrical Efficient
Document status: proposed architecture · build-me phase · 2026–2030 windowThis text describes a proposed transport architecture. It is not a product specification, not a completed system, not a certified interface, and not an industry standard. PBUA, PBUA 2.1, Tri-Domain Fabric, Optical Standard / Wireless Bonded / Electrical Efficient, SE-40, EL-40, PUGC, QD-PBUA Edge Head, and related names are project labels for a concept under design. They are not marks of any standards body and do not imply adoption, interoperability certification, or regulatory approval.Nature of the claim.
“Post-binary” in this document means only that the transport layer may carry richer logical symbols than one bit at a time. Ordinary computers, cameras, microphones, speakers, and displays may remain binary. Binary logic inside chips is not displaced. An A–Z letter is a logical identity used for grouping and session continuity. It is not a promise that English letters are painted as visible rainbow light on a long-haul fabric. Diagram colors, if used, are teaching aids. Working guided lanes, if built, are expected near the telecom infrared window (about 1310–1550 nm), where silicon and silicon-nitride photonics already exist. Visible quantum-dot peaks, if used, are limited in this concept to a short dock or free-space head.Not a mobile generation.
PBUA 2.1 is not 4G, 5G, 6G, or 7G. It is not an ITU, 3GPP, IEEE, or other official generation of mobile radio. Earlier “8G-class” language in this project is a vision label for an ambition (high-radix symbols, multi-wavelength optical bulk, bonded radio, electrical compatibility, and joint scheduling). It is not a licensed telecom generation, not a spectrum allocation, and not a claim of membership in any generational family. Sixth-generation mobile systems remain whatever the competent bodies define toward approximately 2030. This architecture, if built, might sit beside such systems as a helper fabric. It does not become them by naming.Performance, speed, and energy.
All rates, latencies, and energy figures in this document are design envelopes and reasoned hopes, not laboratory results, not field measurements, and not speed-test screenshots. Forward error correction, headers, guard bands, thermal limits, packaging, retries, and host processing will reduce raw capacity. Until a specified bench comparison exists—same movie, same file, against ordinary 5G, Wi-Fi, and cable—speed and joules-per-presented-frame remain unverified.Conceptually, a short, clean optical path carrying a large payload can move more bulk than a typical fifth-generation radio hop and can use fewer joules per delivered bit than a radio climb through air and core network—if unused optical sources sleep and the path is actually optical or short electrical. Conceptually, this is not faster than 5G as a phone in a pocket. Optical flight across a room may be nanoseconds; cameras, encoders, correction, adapters, and screens still live in milliseconds. Everyday 5G latency is often tens of milliseconds; slogan-class 5G air-interface figures are not a substitute for end-to-end media delay. Those two sentences travel together. Tail latency, not only average latency, is the intended comparison metric for flicker and path change. No claim is made that twenty-six lanes produce twenty-six times lower delay.Physics and domain split.
Light and radio are both electromagnetic radiation. The hardware is not the same. This concept does not place a twenty-six-wavelength optical grid into ordinary radio spectrum. Optical guided paths, in this design, use wavelength-division multiplexing and intensity modulation (PAM-4 default; PAM-8 only as a quiet-link bonus). Radio paths use ordinary radio methods (including QAM, MCS, MIMO, OFDMA, carrier aggregation, and multi-link Wi-Fi or cellular). Version 2.1 does not undo that split. Bonded radio means helper, overflow, parity, sync, and continuity with the same session tickets, not a dump of photonic wavelengths onto RF, and not a political claim that a weak beam ends the architecture and leaves the user in “plain 4G/5G.” Session continuity is a design goal, not a demonstrated field behavior.Fewer clean lanes are preferred to a smeared denser grid. Overlapping peaks produce false symbols, retries, heat, and delay. Eight locked infrared data lanes plus one legacy on/off infrared lane is the stated first physical grid; sixteen and twenty-six physical lines are later attempts only if earlier lines stay orthogonal. Logical A–Z identity may time-share or group-share a smaller physical grid. That is policy, not a measured lock.Controllers, buffers, and materials.
