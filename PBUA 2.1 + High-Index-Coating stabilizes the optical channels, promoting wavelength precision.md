#PBUA 2.1 + High-Index-Coating stabilizes the optical channels, promoting wavelength precision and keeping each color distinct.

Optical Standard · Wireless Bonded · Electrical Efficient · Bi lock-plate and optional gain
Proposed · build-me · not a standard · not a productPBUA means Post-Binary Unified Architecture.
It is a proposed way to move pictures, sound, and large computer data without forcing every heavy job 
through one serial stream of ordinary 0s and 1s. The computer can stay ordinary. The camera can stay ordinary.
The speaker and screen can stay ordinary. What changes is the transport layer. Information is organized as an A–Z 
logical alphabet. When light is clean, those letters ride locked wavelengths. When light is weak, the same letters 
keep their names and travel as radio-coded or electrical groups. Version 2 made the dual-domain split honest: optical
hardware is not radio hardware. Version 2.1 kept that physics and refused the extra collapse — the moment a beam sagged,
the session died and the user was told to live in 4G or 5G. Version 2.1’s public name is Optical Standard, Wireless Bonded,
Electrical Efficient. Optical light is the preferred bulk road. Radio is a bonded helper, not only an emergency exit. 
A thin electrical path remains for old cables and low-energy control. SE-40 keeps one A–Z session across all three roads.
EL-40 watches heat, power, errors, and deadlines.The photonic system underneath that story is small and specific.
An A–Z letter is a logical identity, about 4.7 bits before overhead, not a promise that English is painted as a 
visible rainbow on a long-haul cable. The first physical grid is eight locked infrared data lanes plus one dumb
infrared heartbeat that any old intensity detector can read as bright or dark. Those working lanes live near
1310–1550 nm, where silicon and silicon-nitride photonics already exist. Diagrams may color the lanes so people 
can see them. The light itself is not a carnival. Visible quantum-dot peaks, four to eight of them, belong only
on a short dock. Packing stays radix plus WDM plus PAM. PAM-4 is the adult optical mode — four brightness steps, 
two bits per symbol per lane. PAM-8 is a quiet-link bonus. QAM stays on the radio side. Across eight PAM-4 lanes, 
one shared symbol time carries sixteen bits, which is two bytes. Capacity tables that climb toward hundreds of 
gigabits or a terabit-class envelope are hopes for a clean bench, not a speed-test screenshot. Fifth-generation
radio remains the real wide-area phone network. PBUA 2.1 is not 5G, 6G, 7G, or a licensed 8G. On a short, fat,
mostly optical hop it can move a large frame with less waiting in one binary line. In a pocket it cannot replace
cellular. Those two sentences travel together.Why add bismuth at all? Because Version 2.1 already named its optical 
failure mode: smear. If neighboring wavelengths overlap, you do not get more bytes. You get false letters, retries,
heat, and tail delay — the flicker people feel when a beam sags and groups stampede onto radio. Bismuth is not a
fourth engine and not a new alphabet. It is a materials answer to that smear-and-sag problem. Two different 
bismuth tools are allowed. Bismuth oxide, Bi₂O₃, can be deposited as a designed high-index coating. Published
films sit near refractive index 2.4–2.7 with low absorption over a wide window, which is what a filter or
Bragg stack wants when it must keep lane G from bleeding into lane H. A bismuth-doped fiber amplifier is a
different object: a gain coil whose active centers can amplify light in the O, E, and neighboring S bands, 
the same neighborhood the eight IR lanes occupy, instead of only erbium’s C-band. The coating holds a named 
channel still. The amplifier makes that same named channel louder. Neither tool paints twenty-six new colors.
This is therefore an add-on layer over the eight-channel optic, not a replacement of prior 2.1 elements. The
logical sheet music remains A–Z. The physical fabric still starts at eight locked IR lanes. Lane 0, the 
gold single-fiber heartbeat, still asks only bright or dark; if it is dirty, nothing else on the table 
should be trusted. The eight-lane board still carries A–H as locked channels; I–Z time-share those eight 
until sixteen, then maybe twenty-six, physical lines stay clean. SE-40 still tickets every group with 
session, frame, symbol-group, timestamp, and energy class, parks an electron packet for nanoseconds to 
microseconds, writes that packet onto a brief infrared or dock-color burst, and clears the hold. 
Electrons store. Photons carry. There is no photon hard drive. EL-40 still sleeps unused lasers, 
unused gain, and unused PAM-8 DSP. If the host only speaks HDMI or DisplayPort, the photonic palace
turns off. Compatibility must not cost a hidden optical tax. Radio still carries sync, audio, parity,
and overflow with the same tickets after translation into ordinary QAM. It still must not receive a 
dump of twenty-six optical wavelengths. The adapter still rebuilds one framebuffer and one audio buffer 
so the picture and the speaker never learn the alphabet.How the upgrade works in the path is ordinary 
and strict. Light from the eight locked lanes meets a Bi₂O₃ stack whose thicknesses are calculated,
not grown wild. A quarter-wave layer follows d=λ/(4n)d=\lambda/(4n)d=\lambda/(4n)
. With (n) around 2.4–2.5, a 1550 nm quarter-wave is on the order of 150–160 nm. That stack raises 
index contrast, tightens the passband, and keeps each wavelength distinct and smear-free. If a lane 
still sags after filtering, a short BDFA coil may add gain on that same lane. Fiber delay is about
five nanoseconds per meter; a short module stays in the tens of nanoseconds. That is not the latency 
budget of a camera, a decoder, or a radio hop. Average docked optical speed remains the optical engine’s job. 
Bismuth’s job is to stop the expensive pause: fewer false letters, fewer retries, a higher optical SNR floor,
and fewer groups shoved onto radio. Compared with prior 2.1, this is not more felt latency if the coating is
thin and the coil is short. It becomes slower only if someone builds a long spool, a mixed Bi glow, or extra DSP
to rescue a hopper-crystal rainbow.Control of “what color bismuth turns” is the question the upgrade refuses. 
You do not cook a bismuth crystal until it looks like letter K. Natural hopper-crystal color is thin-film interference
from an oxide whose thickness wanders. One lump shows many colors at once. That cannot be assigned. Letter
is assigned in software. Wavelength is locked in hardware. SE-40 says H equals channel 8. A laser, a 
filtered comb line, or a sized quantum-dot pad emits only that peak. Bi₂O₃ does not become H; it 
keeps H from becoming G. On the short dock, Palette 2 may show four to eight visible peaks, one pad,
one driver, one color. The long fabric stays infrared. A peak is legal only after wavelength,
linewidth, guard band, and thermal drift are measured. If two peaks overlap, that letter is not 
assigned. The sick lane is removed and the letter is reticketed onto a clean lane. One dirty color
must not poison the alphabet.So the principles that define this upgrade are few and sharp. 
Keep Version 2’s physics. Keep Version 2.1’s politics of one session. Add bismuth only as 
stabilizer and optional hearing-aid. Do not treat bismuth oxide as a new 26-color PBUA. Do 
not treat radio as a bucket for optical lines. Do not light twenty-six heaters to move a menu.
Prefer eight strong lanes to a smeared prettier grid. Sleep every engine that is not carrying 
ticketed groups. Measure joules per successfully presented frame, including legacy frames. 
Build in order: software tickets; one PAM lane; eight lanes that do not smear; then the Bi₂O₃
lock-plate and, if needed, short O/E/S gain; then SE-40 merge with radio headers; then the 
custom dock head; then the adapter; then a camera-to-speaker demonstration; then a same-movie
bench against 5G, Wi-Fi, and cable. Until that last step exists, speed and energy remain
reasoned hopes.The one-line remainder is this. PBUA 2.1 is still a photonic-first, radio-bonded,
electrically polite proposed fabric in which an A–Z logical session is supposed to survive a 
change of physical engine. The bismuth upgrade is a yes: add Bi₂O₃ over the eight optical channels 
so each locked color stays itself, and add a short BDFA only to keep a named lane loud. 
It is a no if bismuth is asked to recreate the alphabet. The letters were already PBUA’s.
The coating only keeps them from smearing.
